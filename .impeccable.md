# Design Context

Loaded by the `/design:*` and `/impeccable` skills before any design, UI, or frontend work. Keep this in sync with the "Design Context" section of `CLAUDE.md` and `AGENTS.md`.

## Product

A documentation site for a five-session Claude training program ("Cloud AI Training"), with a three-session Codex & ChatGPT track as the secondary path. Claude stays first on the homepage, Start Here, and the sidebar. Static Next.js export on GitHub Pages. Light and dark mode are both first-class.

## Users

Engineers, mostly cloud, container, and IAM engineers, using the site in two overlapping modes:

- **Learning mode**: going through the sessions in order, one per week, often re-reading on a laptop or phone after the live session.
- **Reference mode**: jumping in mid-task to look up a pattern, a permission rule, or a setting. They land cold on a page from search and need to orient in seconds.

Design must serve both: structured enough to read end to end, scannable enough to land on any page.

## Brand personality

Three words: **confident, crafted, program**. The voice is direct, technical, mildly opinionated, written by engineers for engineers. Not marketing copy, not corporate, not whimsical. No exclamation marks. Humor only when precise.

## Aesthetic direction

**Bold training brand built on docs-grade structure.** Identity-forward like a course microsite (Josh Comeau, Epic Web), organized like Stripe, Tailwind, or Linear docs.

- One interaction color per theme, reached through the `violet` scale, is the primary signal and the only interaction color: active nav, primary CTA, links, eyebrows, info callouts. In light mode it is Tomorrow's purple (`#8959a8`, text and hover a step darker); in dark mode it is Vercel's bright blue (`#49aeff`, hover `#79c4ff`). The original brand violet (`#6240E8`) stays in `@theme` but is not shown while both themes override the scale.
- Display face: Bricolage Grotesque for H1, H2, hero headlines, and roadmap numerals. Body: Inter. The identity lives in the headings; the reading stays neutral. Landing pages carry the loudest type: a fluid display headline (40px to 80px) and, on a session landing page, the session numeral in the session hue as a chapter opener. Lesson and reference pages keep the restrained prose H1. Below it the prose scale steps down by about a quarter each time: H1 2.5rem and H2 1.625rem in the display face, H3 1.25rem in Inter at the size of a card title, body 1rem. A sub-section heading must be larger than a bold lead-in, which stays at body size.
- Page surfaces take each terminal theme's background as it is: white in light (`--color-paper`), `#101010` in dark (`--color-ink`). Code blocks sit on the theme background in light and on true black in dark.
- **Light mode is the Ghostty Tomorrow terminal theme**: white page, charcoal text (`#4d4d4c`), black headings, and its six ANSI hues as the accents. The palette is declared once as `--term-*` under `:root` in `tailwind.css`; every light token maps to an entry, and the `zinc` and `violet` scales are re-pointed at it. The theme has no pink, so the six accent roles map onto its hues one to one with its cyan in the pink role (session 4). Most of its hues are too light to read as text on white, so each has a `-text` variant (same hue and chroma, lower OKLCH lightness) that holds 4.5:1 on every tint; badge fills use that variant under white text. The card surface (`#f5f5f5`) and the secondary text gray (`#646463`) are mixes of the theme's grays.
- **Dark mode is the Ghostty Vercel terminal theme**: near-black page (`--color-ink`, `#101010`), white text (`#fafafa`), and its vivid ANSI colors as the accents. The palette is declared once as `--term-*` under `.dark` in `tailwind.css`; every dark token maps to an entry, and the `zinc`, `violet`, and `white` scales are re-pointed at it. The theme has six hues and no purple, so the six accent roles map onto them one to one: purple (interaction, session 3) is its blue, blue (aqua in light, session 1) is its cyan, pink (session 4) is its magenta, and green, amber, and red are its green, yellow, and red. Text uses the bright variant of each hue; the plain variants are for bars and tints. Fills carry page-colored text (`--on-accent`), the way a terminal draws its cursor. The card surface (`#1c1c1c`), raised surface (`#282828`), and secondary text gray (`#d1d1d1`) are mixes of the theme's grays. Code blocks sit on the theme's true black. The cursor magenta (`#f32882`) is the text caret and the selection blue (`#005be7`) is text selection.
- One card surface everywhere: hairline `--surface-border`, `--radius-card`, no resting shadow, no colored top bars, left-aligned content. A row's color goes on its badge, not on an edge of the card. `Callout` is the tinted variant of the same shape: the variant's tint, with the hairline in the variant's hue.

**Explicitly avoid**: purple-gradient-on-white heros, glassmorphism, dark-mode-first with glowing accents, gradient text, identical icon-heading-text card grids, thick colored borders on one side of a card, corporate-enterprise blandness, whimsy without purpose.

## Color rules

- **Violet**: interaction and brand only.
- **Risk scale** (shared by permission components and callouts): green runs freely, amber asks first, red is blocked or highest risk.
- **Neutral** (`--brand-neutral-*`, a mid gray from each theme): comparison cards and tiers where color would imply a meaning it doesn't have. Violet marks the destination or the default in a set.
- Semantic diagram colors (spawn arrows, task lists) stay inside the diagram that defines them and carry a legend.
- **Session hues** (`--session-1..5`: the text-safe blue, green, purple, and pink roles, plus a deeper step of the interaction color for session 5; each theme maps the roles onto its own hues, so a session's hue differs between light and dark) appear only on text, icons, and markers: hero eyebrow and session numeral, session crumb, pillar-card icons, sidebar marker, comparison-card titles and bullets, `SessionOutline` number chips, the `SessionComplete` check, and the `TrainingRoadmap` numerals and "You are here" chip. Outside a session's pages the eyebrow and sidebar marker fall back to violet.
- **Contrast rule**: every text token must reach 4.5:1 on the darkest tint it can sit on, in both themes. `python3 -I scripts/color-audit.py` prints the ratios for the session hues, brand text, body and muted text, and info and warning callout text on the page, card, and five tint surfaces, flagging a pair under 4.5:1 with `~` and under 3:1 with `!`. It does not cover `--theme-heading`, `--theme-text-subtle`, error and success callout text, or the pink tint, and it always exits 0. Adjust lightness in OKLCH, keep hue and chroma.

## Design principles

1. **Docs-grade bones, program-grade skin.** Scannability and nav predictability first; identity shows through typography and purposeful violet on top of that skeleton.
2. **Earn every decoration.** Borders, shadows, gradients, motion must serve comprehension, hierarchy, or brand. One reveal per page load, no infinite animations.
3. **Violet with restraint.** Reserve it for real primary moments.
4. **Both themes, equal care.** Review every surface in light and dark; neither is an afterthought.
5. **Write like an engineer talking to an engineer.** Specific nouns over vague adjectives.

## Page rhythm

Content pages: `H1 → ReadTime (3+ min pages only) → one-line tagline → body → "## Where to go next"`. Session landing pages: `PageHero (numeral, eyebrow, title, subtitle) → SessionOutline → TrainingRoadmap`; track landing pages are the same without the numeral, and without the outline where the roadmap already lists each session's topics (the Claude track); the home page is `PageHero → HomeActions → HomeRoadmap → HomeSections`. Running text stops at `--measure` (38rem, about 75 characters a line); tables, code, cards, and diagrams keep the full column. One Callout per page at most. Components get their vertical rhythm from the `.prose` rules in `tailwind.css`; never add `<br />` spacers. Every card grid stacks to one column below `sm:`.
